A very common piece of advice you will hear is get an internship. Internships are how you will start to gain experience in the field so when you graduate you already have some work experience. However applying for your first internship can feel very overwhelming and confusing. This guide covers the best practices for hunting for referrals,optimizing your resume, searching for positions, and preparing for coding interviews.
## Hunting for Referals
Referrals are extremely useful to set you apart from other candidates and in some cases skipping ATS.Research shows referred candidates get interviews at 40-65% rates versus 2-8% for cold applications [^1].  

The best way to start off your hunt is by reaching out to any connections you have that are currently working in Software Development if you don't have any connections look for huski alumni you can connect with. Inquire about their company's internship program and request a referral if their company allows its employees to refer internship candidates. 
## Optimizing Your Resume 
The contents of your resume will be the primary factor in determining if you will be considered as a candidate. After you apply your resume will be scanned by ATS (Applicant Tracking System) looking for relevant keywords and skills if you don't have a sufficient match you will not pass ATS meaning you application will never be looked at by a human meaning you will be rejected by an automated system [^2]. 

The best way to optimize your resume is by adding keywords directly from job listings by scanning multiple listings and identifying reoccurring coding languages, frameworks, and tools being listed and if you have any experience with those adding them to your resume's sills section. If you don't have experience with a language, framework or tool you are seeing its a good sign you should learn the basics so you can include it as a skill. Use a single column format and don't use graphics as those will cause ATS to miss parts of your resume.   
## Searching for Positions
To secure an internship you will need to cast a wide net as internships are highly competitive. Applications for summer internships can open a full 9-10 months before the start date and its best to get your applications in early. As a husky student you have the advantage of being able to look for opportunities on Handshake this only lists jobs that are searching for husky students spisificly. 

You should be applying to as many internships as you can as early as you can but if you miss the peak window don't give up your search. Be sure to look at multiple job posting websited and not just one.
## Preparing for Coding Interviews
Coding interviews act as a way for a company to see how you tackle problems in real time and design and implement a solution. You should be explaining what you are planning to do as you write your answer. The 4 most important criteria the interviewer is looking for is communication, problem solving, technical competency, and testing.

Don't just dive into Leetcode to practice as alone it wont help you much instead, "Pick a good programming language to use, plan your time and tackle topics and questions in order of importance, combine studying and practicing for a single topic, accompany practice with coding interview cheat sheets to internalize the must-dos and must-remembers, prepare a good self introduction and final questions, try out mock coding interviews (with Google and Facebook engineers)" [^3].
